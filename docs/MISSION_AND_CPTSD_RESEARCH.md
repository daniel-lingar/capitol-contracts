# Why 17.5 Drew Me to This Mission

**Personal motivation and research context — Daniel Bret Lingar**  
**Sources checked: October 14, 2026**

## My personal connection

I am not a Veteran. I live with C-PTSD, and I know from my own experience how hard it can be to live with it.

When I encountered figures about C-PTSD among Veterans and the statistic of 17.5 Veteran suicide deaths per day, the suffering behind those numbers felt personal to me. I had not lived their military experiences, but I recognized some of the difficulty of living with trauma. That recognition drew me toward this mission.

**“I am building this to lower 17.5 to 0.”**

Zero expresses the goal of no Veteran lost to suicide. It is an aspiration, not a promise or a measured outcome of my work.

## What research supports

A 2023 study by Robinson, McGlinchey, and Armour examined 638 UK Armed Forces veterans living in Northern Ireland. Probable C-PTSD was significantly associated with depression, anxiety, lifetime suicidal thoughts, and lifetime suicide attempts.

The study used screening questionnaires and data collected at one point in time. It did not establish causation, follow participants for subsequent suicide deaths, or represent all U.S. Veterans. Current symptoms and lifetime suicidal experiences also do not establish which came first.

This supports a connection between C-PTSD and suicidality in a veteran sample. It does not establish how much C-PTSD contributed to the U.S. national suicide count.

[Read the study: Robinson et al. (2023), DOI 10.1080/20008066.2023.2212551](https://doi.org/10.1080/20008066.2023.2212551)

The VA identifies PTSD-related vulnerabilities relevant to suicide risk, including sleep difficulties, defeat and entrapment, social withdrawal, and difficulties regulating emotions. These overlap with difficulties relevant to C-PTSD. That overlap provides context; it does not prove the cause of an individual death.

[VA National Center for PTSD: The Relationship Between Trauma, PTSD, and Suicide](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/cooccurring/suicide_ptsd.asp)

## What 17.5 means—and the newer figure

The VA's February 5, 2026 announcement reported 6,398 Veteran suicide deaths in 2023, averaging 17.5 per day. It counts Veteran suicide deaths overall, not deaths attributed to C-PTSD.

The newer September 2026 VA report records 6,488 Veteran suicide deaths in 2024, averaging 17.7 per day.

**17.5 remains the historical figure that drew me to the mission. It is not the latest daily average.**

- [VA announcement covering 2023 data](https://news.va.gov/press-room/va-releases-annual-veteran-suicide-prevention-report-updated-with-2023-data/)
- [2026 VA annual report covering 2024 data](https://www.mentalhealth.va.gov/MENTALHEALTH/docs/data-sheets/2026/CMR_2026_National_Veteran_Suicide_Prevention_Annual_Report.pdf)

## What remains unknown

The national death count does not tell us how many of those Veterans had C-PTSD or how much it contributed to their deaths. The VA report does not provide a separate C-PTSD category in its diagnostic analyses.

The VA explains that ICD-11 recognizes C-PTSD as a separate diagnosis, while DSM-5 includes complex trauma responses within its broader PTSD diagnosis. PTSD statistics therefore cannot simply be relabeled as C-PTSD statistics.

[VA National Center for PTSD: Complex PTSD—History and Definitions](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/essentials/complex_ptsd.asp)

Suicidal thoughts, suicide attempts, and suicide deaths are different outcomes. Evidence about one cannot automatically be treated as evidence about the others.

## How this relates to my work

My experience explains why I care. Research supports taking C-PTSD and trauma-related distress seriously when considering suicide risk among Veterans. The national statistic shows the scale of loss; it does not establish a single cause.

WRH and The Rescue Boat focus on plain-language education about survival patterns and reducing shame and misunderstanding. This research does not validate those materials as suicide-prevention interventions. WRH remains development-stage, non-clinical education with no current delivery authorization.
